### Urgent responses

In academia, unlike medical emergencies or other crises, most situations do not require immediate attention, even those that may seem urgent. If you face challenges, such as a technical issue during an exam, here's how to handle it:

* **Document the Problem**: If you encounter difficulties with Blackboard, take screenshots and note any error messages.
* **Contact Me**: Send a direct message on Discord or an email explaining the issue.
* **Await My Response**: I will address the problem during normal working hours. My methods for resolving the issue, such as granting time extensions or resets, are designed to ensure that you are not disadvantaged. **Rest assured, I will assist you as soon as I'm available.**

### Online quiz and exam reset policy

I make it a point to remind students of this policy during lecture before each exam. If you experience internet issues, technical difficulties, accidentally open the exam, or face other non-academic distractions during a quiz or exam, follow these steps:

1. **Don't Panic**: Stay calm and do not rush.
2. **Stop and Exit:** Close the quiz or exam without viewing additional questions.
3. **Contact Me Immediately**: Preferably via Discord, but email is also fine. Do not expect an immediate response, I will contact you when I am available.

**How I Handle Different Situations**

1. **Less than 10% of the questions**: I can reset the quiz or exam for a fresh start.
2. **Between 10% and 90% of the questions**: I will likely create a custom quiz or exam covering only the questions you haven't seen.
3. **100% of the questions (rushed to complete)**: I can offer a second attempt, but your final score will be the average of both attempts.

**Group Quizzes**: Short assessments to test knowledge on recent topics.

**Exams**: Cumulative assessments covering course material.

* Cumulative, mid-term/final.
* 100 points total (50 points/75 minutes each part).
* Completed individually.

### Group quiz rules and format

**Format of the Quiz**

* 20 points total in 20-34 questions
  * 1 point for each multiple-choice
  * ½ point for each choice in a matching question
  * Limited calculations; not testing calculator usage
* Approximately 20-30 minutes for the quiz (time varies by content)
  * About 1-1½ minutes per question to prevent collaboration or excessive research
  * Not designed to be rushed
  * Backtracking allowed (may change if cheating suspected)
  * Questions are in random order

**Individual Quiz**

* Conducted electronically via Blackboard
* Opens 20 hours before the start of class
* Must be completed BEFORE class

**Group Quiz**

* Form groups of 3-4 students
  * Sometimes students pick, sometimes random
  * May take a while to organize break out groups
  * I try to maintain the same groups if they are working well
  * Contact me if there are other students you want in your group
* Collaboration over Zoom Breakout Room/Face-to-Face
* Quiz done electronically over Blackboard
  * Same or similar questions to the Individual Quiz
  * Only one student per group will submit the group's answers
* The minimum group score is 18 out of 20 points
* Group scores are curved for hard quizzes

**Final Score**: Average of Individual and Group Scores (e.g., (14 \+ 20)/2 \= 17 points)

### Graded percentage vs total percentage

The **Graded Percentage** shows your score based only on the work that has been graded. It does not include assignments that you have not submitted or items that still need grading. This percentage is useful when the instructor has not yet graded items. It gives you an idea of your current grade, focusing only on the work you have completed and that has been scored.

The **Total Percentage** includes all the items in the grade book, as listed on Blackboard. This includes every assignment, quiz, and exam, regardless of whether they are done, not submitted, or still waiting to be graded. In this percentage, any work that you have unfinished or have not submitted is counted as zero until it is completed and graded. The Total Percentage is more representative when you have assignments that you have not yet attempted. It provides an overview of your performance, factoring in both completed and pending assignments.

In summary, the Total Percentage will be equal to or lower than the Graded Percentage, as it includes unattempted and ungraded assignments. The Graded Percentage is a better measure for students who are up to date with their work, reflecting only graded items. In contrast, the Total Percentage provides a better overview for students with incomplete assignments, as it incorporates all potential grades.

### Common criticisms and my responses to discussion grades

I am fully aware of the drawbacks to this system, but I really want to encourage discussion. Without discussion, you might as well view the lecture on YouTube. There are five main complaints about this system, I am aware that they exist, and I actively try to reduce their impact.

1. **Students ask silly or stupid questions to get marks:** I have to encourage all questions and comments, because otherwise more introverted students might feel discouraged from asking a perceived stupid question.
2. **The instructor favors certain students:** I am aware that I tend to give marks to some students more than others, and I actively try to avoid this through mark shaming (discouraging over-eager students from getting more) and placing limits on the maximum marks, but alas, I am human. I appreciate feedback, so please feel free to report to me any issues of favoritism.
3. **The distribution of marks is inconsistent:** Some great questions go unrewarded while poor questions often get rewarded. Again, I am human; if you feel that you were overlooked and missed a mark (or more), please come up to me after class. I am very forgiving and will likely apologize and reward you for it.
4. **Creates a competitive environment for discussion that is overly stressful:** You should not feel that it is a competition; if you speak only once per week, you can still get a decent grade. Remember, using the cube root ensures that the more marks you get, the less value each additional mark has.

### Scoring of discussion grades

During the discussion, I will award discussion marks to students for stimulating discussion, asking thoughtful questions, or correctly answering posed questions. **Discussion marks are not directly equal to points in the grade book.**

At the end of the semester, I will use the fourth-highest discussion mark count as the baseline for 100%. Then, I will apply the cube root to each student's discussion marks ratio to determine their distribution. A person with zero discussion marks will receive a minimal amount of points, and the remaining discussion marks will be scaled up to the maximum points. Using the cube root ensures that as you accumulate more marks, each additional mark has less impact.

There is a limit of 2 discussion marks per hour of the course, so for a 3-hour session, the limit is 6 discussion marks. This rule prevents students from hoarding discussion marks. Discussion marks may also be rewarded for showing up to class on time or staying for the entire class, but this will only apply if attendance becomes a problem.

### No make-up for discussion marks

I do not offer make-up opportunities for missed discussion marks. The discussion marks system rewards meaningful participation throughout the semester. Missed opportunities to participate in class discussions cannot be made up later. However, the grading scale accounts for different levels of participation. Consistent engagement throughout the semester is encouraged, but active participation in at least half of the class sessions, or partial participation in all class sessions, still results in a high grade. By rule, more than half of the students in the class will earn an A or A-. This system ensures fairness and highlights the importance of contributing during live class discussions and contributions from all students add to the collective learning experience.

### Lab attendance and weekly preparation score

**Lab attendance is required.** Regular attendance in lab class is essential for your success in this course. Labs are hands-on, collaborative, and require an extensive, complex, and unique setup. As a result, make-up labs are not offered. Your attendance and preparation will be assessed weekly.

Deductions may be made for not bringing your lab coat, not wearing lab goggles, having exposed ankles, failing to tie back long hair, or violating other safety rules. Points may also be deducted for not having a copy of the lab manual, not preparing your lab notebook, or arriving late. Additional deductions may apply for failing to lock your drawer, leaving out your drawer key, leaving behind an unclean bench, or any other clean-up issues. Students who are fully prepared, follow safety protocols, and participate actively will earn the full preparation score for that week.

**There are no make-up labs**, so attending your scheduled session is the only way to earn credit. To balance fairness, accountability, and the reality that illness, emergencies, and conflicts do occur, the following grading system will be used for missed labs:

| Absence Type | Score | Included inTotal Points |
| ----- | :---: | :---: |
| First communicated | N/A | No |
| Second communicated | 3/5 | Yes |
| Third communicated | 1/5 | Yes |
| Fourth or higher communicated | 0/5 | Yes |
| First *uncommunicated* | 0/5 | Yes |
| Second or higher *uncommunicated* | -2/5\* | Yes |

*\* Negative scores mean points are deducted from the total earned in other weeks.*

Absences fall into two categories: communicated and uncommunicated. A **communicated absence** is when a student contacts the instructor before the start of lab to explain they will not be attending. This includes illness, family emergencies, travel conflicts, or personal matters. While communicated absences still result in a reduced or missing preparation score (see table above), they are treated more leniently than absences with no communication. An **uncommunicated absence** is when a student misses lab class without notifying the instructor in advance. These will result in greater point deductions and may impact your standing in the course (see table above).

### Title IX and sexual misconduct policy

Roosevelt University cares greatly about the health and wellbeing of our students, staff, faculty, and guests to our campuses. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between an A and an A-?**

* An A- grade is earned when your points percentage falls between 90.0% and 91.9%, an A is any grade 92.0% or above.

**How are discussion marks earned for remote classes?**

* First, Discussion Marks are mostly based on the chat. Not all comments in the chat count, such as "hi", "thanks", or "yes". You can earn up to 6 discussion marks from the chat.
* Second, answering polls does NOT count. It is not easy for me to obtain that data
* Third, being present in the Zoom meeting when class time starts can earn up to 2 discussion marks for being on-time to class.

**What should I do if I face a technical issue during a quiz or an exam?**

* Document the problem by taking screenshots and noting any errors.
* Contact Dr. Voss immediately via Discord or email.
* Await a response during normal working hours; Dr. Voss will provide solutions such as time extensions or exam resets.

**Can I request an extension for assignments or exams?**

* Yes, if you experience unexpected circumstances such as illness or personal emergencies, contact Dr. Voss immediately. Extensions are granted based on individual circumstances, with open communication being crucial.

**How are group quizzes conducted?**

* Group quizzes are formed with 3-4 students, conducted electronically via Blackboard.
* Groups may be self-selected or randomly assigned.
* Only one student per group submits the group's answers.
* The final score is the average of individual and group quiz scores.

**How do I contact Dr. Voss?**

* Primary contact method: Discord server.
* Secondary contact method: Email (nvoss@roosevelt.edu)
* Office hours: Available over Zoom (check syllabus for specific times).

**How does the attendance policy affect my grade?**

* Attendance is crucial for success. While not formally tracked, participation points are awarded during class sessions.
* Falling below two-thirds attendance may jeopardize your ability to pass the course. International students must attend 80% of classes to maintain their visa status.

**Can I withdraw from the class after the official withdrawal date?**

* Late withdrawals are possible by submitting a "Petition for a Late W Grade" due to extraordinary non-academic circumstances, with approval required from the instructor, department chair, and dean.

**What is the difference between "Graded Percentage" and "Total Percentage" on Blackboard?**

* Graded Percentage only reflects your score on work that has been completed and graded. Total Percentage includes all work in the grade book, counting unattempted or unsubmitted assignments as zero.
* For a full explanation, see the **Graded Percentage vs Total Percentage** section.
