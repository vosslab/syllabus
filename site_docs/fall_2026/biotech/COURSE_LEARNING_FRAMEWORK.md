## Learning Objectives:

Students completing this course will have achieved:

- **Identity of Modern Biotechnology:** Develop a clear understanding of biotechnology as a
  discipline that manipulates organisms, cells, genes, proteins, or other biological systems and
  distinguish it from traditional biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, supplements, bioengineering,
  and medical devices.
- **Breadth and Context of Biotechnology Applications:** Gain familiarity with medical,
  agricultural, environmental, industrial, genomic, protein-based, and synthetic-biology
  applications and understand how historical developments shaped the modern field.
- **Scientific Mechanisms and Feasibility:** Learn to trace a proposed product from its organism,
  cell, gene, protein, pathway, or molecular method to its intended function and judge whether the
  mechanism is plausible with current or realistically developing technology.
- **Evaluation of Current Evidence:** Use primary literature, biotechnology news, and case studies
  to determine what evidence supports a technology, what questions remain unresolved, and how a
  rapidly changing field should update its claims.
- **Innovation and Responsible Commercialization:** Connect scientific discovery with users,
  market need, competition, intellectual property, regulation, development milestones, investment,
  and the ethical and social consequences of bringing a biotechnology product into the world.
- **Evidence-Based Biotechnology Communication:** Gain experience converting complex science into
  focused talking points, an executive summary, informative visuals, a persuasive oral pitch, and
  a collaborative company proposal without sacrificing scientific accuracy.

## Course Learning Outcomes:

Students completing this course will be able to:

- Distinguish modern biotechnology from traditional fermentation or breeding, conventional drug
  manufacturing, supplements and traditional remedies, bioengineering, and medical devices by
  identifying whether and how a biological system is manipulated.
- Explain how DNA and genomics technologies, protein biotechnology, the central dogma, and
  synthetic-biology methods can create or improve medical, agricultural, environmental, or
  industrial products and processes.
- Locate and evaluate primary literature and credible current sources to determine what evidence
  supports a biotechnology claim, identify the organism, cell, gene, protein, pathway, or molecular
  method responsible for its function, and judge whether the mechanism is technically plausible.
- Analyze a biotechnology case from scientific, ethical, regulatory, environmental, social,
  intellectual-property, and economic perspectives and explain how those constraints influence
  development into a usable product.
- Generate three distinct modern-biotechnology company ideas, then develop one into an executive
  summary that defines the problem, product, biological mechanism, evidence, users, competition,
  advantage, and major development milestones.
- Communicate and defend biotechnology through a one-slide, 60-second talking point, a visual
  company pitch, and an integrated group presentation and business plan, using instructor and peer
  feedback to improve the science, scope, and explanation.

## Learning Goals:

Overall, this course aims to accomplish:

- **Applied Biotechnology Practice:** Shift from memorizing a survey of technologies to using prior
  molecular-biology knowledge to evaluate cases, solve problems, and design plausible applications.
- **Scientific Specificity:** Replace vague promises with a clearly defined problem, an identifiable
  biological mechanism, credible evidence, measurable development steps, and honest limits on what
  the technology can do.
- **Current and Critical Awareness:** Build the habit of following emerging biotechnology and
  questioning the evidence, novelty, feasibility, and unstated assumptions behind scientific news
  and commercial claims.
- **Responsible Innovation Literacy:** Understand that a strong biological idea must navigate
  regulation, intellectual property, competition, market need, technical risk, financing, ethical
  judgment, and effects on health, access, society, and the environment before it becomes a useful
  product.
- **Professional Scientific Communication:** Develop the ability to adapt the same complex idea for
  a 60-second explanation, a visual presentation, an executive summary, a scientific discussion,
  and a business plan.
- **Collaborative Career Preparation:** Strengthen peer feedback, shared planning, scientific
  review, conflict resolution, and responsibility for one coherent team product while preparing
  for graduate research, industry, policy, entrepreneurship, and other biotechnology careers.
