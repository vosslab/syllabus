# Talking points: one slide, one minute

Talking points are recurring, one-slide, 60-second assignments. You will research one useful angle
on an assigned topic and teach it to the class. The short format forces you to decide what matters
most; the reward is a course filled with more current examples and perspectives than any one person
could bring alone.

Talking-point assignments are worth 72 points across the semester. I will announce each set's topic
assignments and deadlines and provide the shared-presentation link.

## Required workflow

1. Receive one or two assigned topics during the preceding class.
2. Research the topic using the textbook and appropriate outside sources.
3. Edit your assigned slide in the shared presentation before it is locked for class.
4. Prepare a focused explanation that fits within 60 seconds.
5. Present in your own words and answer reasonable questions about the broader topic.
6. Listen to the other presentations and contribute useful questions or connections.

At least two students may share a topic. Listen to the other presenter and add a different example,
mechanism, implication, or perspective instead of repeating the same facts.

## Slide requirements

Your slide must:

- preserve the provided theme and assigned slide location;
- use several relevant images and enough text to orient the audience;
- use short phrases and keywords rather than complete sentences or paragraphs;
- define the topic, connect it to biotechnology, or present a recent biotechnology example;
- reflect research beyond simply copying the textbook;
- include your own selection, interpretation, or perspective;
- minimize jargon and define necessary technical terms; and
- place detailed speaking notes in the presenter notes rather than on the visible slide.

Movies and animated images are discouraged because they can consume most of the minute. Use them
only when they materially improve the explanation. External audio is not part of the assignment.

## Presentation requirements

Your presentation must:

- stay focused and fit within 60 seconds;
- make one idea understandable and memorable;
- explain the topic at the level of the class;
- use your own words instead of directly quoting an article, website, or textbook;
- use notes if helpful without reading the visible slide;
- show genuine interest in why the topic matters; and
- prepare you to answer questions about material you could not fit into the minute.

Video is optional during a video-conference presentation. Your audience should finish knowing one
important idea and why it matters to biotechnology.

## Scoring

Each assigned talking point is scored from 0 to 6 points. A score of 4 means you completed the
assigned slide and presentation and met the stated requirements. Scores of 5 or 6 recognize work
that is not merely longer, but more effective through research, visual choices, explanation, or
insight.

| Points | Standard | Evidence in the work |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 6 | Exceptional | Meets every requirement and adds a memorable, well-supported insight or connection |
| 5 | Strong | Meets every requirement with especially effective research, visuals, or delivery |
| 4 | Complete | Provides the required slide and presentation at the expected level |
| 3 | Developing | Provides useful work but has a clear problem with preparation, organization, or clarity |
| 2 | Incomplete | Misses several requirements or leaves important parts unfinished |
| 1 | Minimal | Shows little preparation or communicates very little usable information |
| 0 | Missing | Leaves the assigned slide blank or does not complete the assignment |

## Separate discussion credit

Talking-point points and discussion marks evaluate different work. Your assigned slide and
presentation earn talking-point points. Asking a thoughtful question, answering a question, or
adding a useful connection to another presentation can earn separate discussion marks. A greeting,
agreement, or acknowledgment by itself is not a discussion contribution.

See [Discussion marks](DISCUSSION_MARKS.md) for participation and scoring details. If you miss the
class in which topics are selected, contact me to request an available topic before presenting.
