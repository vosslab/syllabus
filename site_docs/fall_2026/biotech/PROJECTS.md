# Biotechnology company projects

These projects are the centerpiece of the course. You will turn biotechnology knowledge into a
hypothetical company twice: first by developing and pitching your own proposal, then by joining a
team to build one proposal into a deeper scientific presentation and business plan. The challenge
is to make an ambitious idea scientifically specific, plausible, and worth pursuing.

Follow the directions and links provided for each project component for its deadline, submission
method, format limits, and any required forms or detailed rubric. The expectations on this page
remain the same throughout the project.

## Individual company project

Your individual project is worth 88 points. It moves from several possible ideas to one supported
proposal and a recruitment pitch. Your goal is to convince classmates that the problem matters,
the biotechnology can work, and the company is worth developing during the group project.

### Project idea requirements

Each idea must propose a product or process that uses a biological system, organism, cell, gene,
protein, or other biological component. A generic device, conventional drug, supplement, or
traditional remedy is not automatically a modern-biotechnology project. If your first idea falls
outside the course, use the feedback to redesign it around molecular or cellular biotechnology.

For each idea, you should be able to answer:

- What specific problem does the company address, and who or what is affected?
- What product or process will the company create?
- Which organism, cell, gene, protein, pathway, or molecular method makes it work?
- Can the proposal be pursued with current or realistically developing technology?
- Who would use or purchase it?
- What existing product, method, or indirect alternative already addresses the problem?
- Why would your approach be more effective, practical, affordable, or useful?

This is a library-research project; you are not expected to conduct laboratory experiments. You
are expected to replace vague claims with specific biological mechanisms and credible evidence.

### Executive summary requirements

The executive summary must stand on its own as a concise company proposal. A reader should be able
to identify:

- the company and product;
- the specific problem, its importance, and the people or systems affected;
- the relevant scientific background;
- how the biotechnology works, including specific biological components or methods;
- why the proposed solution fits the problem and is scientifically plausible;
- the intended users or market and how the company could sustain the product;
- direct and indirect competition and the proposal's advantage; and
- the major milestones needed to develop the idea.

Support important claims with appropriate sources and quantitative evidence when it is available.
Write for scientifically trained readers who may not know your specialty. Clear organization,
specific language, accurate science, readable formatting, and genuine belief in the proposal all
matter. Follow the shared
[academic-integrity policy](../shared/policies/ACADEMIC_INTEGRITY.md) when using AI or other help.

### Company pitch requirements

The pitch recruits classmates to the group project; it is not a compressed technical lecture. It
must:

- identify you, the company, and the proposed product;
- establish the problem before presenting the product as the solution;
- explain enough science to make the proposal credible;
- show what distinguishes the product from competing approaches;
- use readable visuals, short phrases, and cited sources;
- tell one connected story with a beginning, middle, and end;
- use your own spoken words instead of reading the slides; and
- prepare you to answer questions about the proposal.

The instructor and your classmates score the pitch separately. Strong work combines scientific
depth with clarity and visible enthusiasm: make the audience understand both how the idea works and
why you care about it.

## Group company project

The group project is worth 91 points. Your team selects one company from the individual proposals
and may revise its name, product, market, or scientific strategy. The group version must go beyond
the original pitch by adding scientific depth, evidence, milestones, management, and investment
logic.

### Teamwork requirements

The presentation and business plan are shared products, not separate pieces placed beside one
another at the end. Your team is expected to:

- begin both products early and keep them in shared documents;
- agree on the company's terminology, evidence, visual style, and strategy;
- have at least two members review each major section;
- use roles such as coordinator, researcher, formatter, and editor when useful;
- make every member responsible for both scientific work and communication; and
- revise the final products so they read and sound like one company created them.

Peer contribution scores reflect the quality and reliability of each person's work, including
research, writing, review, coordination, problem solving, and support for the team. A name on the
final submission is not evidence of an equal contribution.

### Group presentation requirements

The group presentation must:

- establish the problem and its importance;
- explain the biotechnology in enough depth for the class;
- identify the biological components, methods, and evidence supporting the product;
- introduce the product as a solution to the stated problem;
- compare it with direct and indirect competitors;
- explain the market, development milestones, and path forward;
- give every member a meaningful speaking role; and
- tell a connected story rather than a sequence of unrelated individual speeches.

The science is the center of the presentation. The instructor and classmates score the presentation
separately. Timing, submission, and peer-evaluation procedures will be announced before the
presentation.

### Business plan requirements

The business plan must make a complete, evidence-based case for the company. It must address:

- an executive summary that can stand on its own;
- the problem and relevant business and scientific background;
- a specific explanation of the product and its biotechnology;
- market need, product niche, and direct and indirect competition;
- development milestones and the evidence needed to reach them;
- the management team and how each person's expertise supports the milestones;
- an investment and financial summary; and
- professional organization, figures, tables, citations, and writing.

Use the section order, formatting rules, and scoring criteria announced for the business plan.
Before the deadline, check the uploaded document to make sure format conversion did not alter its
pages, figures, tables, or references.
