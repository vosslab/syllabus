## Learning Objectives

Students completing this course will have achieved:

- Evidence-based problem solving, quantitative reasoning, collaboration, and clear scientific
  communication rather than isolated factual recall.

## Course Learning Outcomes

Students completing this course will be able to:

1. Distinguish mutation types and analyze their effects on genetic disorders.
2. Explain DNA structure and base pairing to a non-specialist audience.
3. Apply genotyping methods including RFLP, VNTR, STR, and SNP analysis.
4. Determine restriction-enzyme cut locations and predict DNA fragments.
5. Interpret gel-electrophoresis evidence in paternity and forensic problems.
6. Evaluate ethical implications of DNA profiling and genetic information.
7. Explain how human genetic variation relates to ancestry and disease susceptibility.
8. Relate mitosis and meiosis to chromosome inheritance and genetic variation.
9. Apply Mendelian principles to predict inheritance patterns.
10. Solve inheritance problems involving multiple alleles, gene interactions, and polygenic traits.
11. Explain how Morgan's experiments connected genes to chromosomes.
12. Analyze inheritance of X-linked genes.
13. Calculate recombination frequencies and construct gene maps.
14. Construct gene maps from tetrad-analysis data.
15. Assess the effects of polyploidy, aneuploidy, and chromosome rearrangements.
16. Use chi-square tests to evaluate genetic hypotheses.
17. Compare natural selection, genetic drift, and gene flow as mechanisms of evolution.

## Learning Goals

Overall, this course aims to accomplish:

- Examination of inheritance through classical and molecular genetics, with an emphasis on problem
  solving, experimental evidence, gene interactions, chromosome behavior, and genetic mapping.
