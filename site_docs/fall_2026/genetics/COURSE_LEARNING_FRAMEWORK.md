## Learning Objectives:

Students completing this course will have achieved:

- **Foundational Understanding of Genetics:** Gain a solid foundation in key genetic concepts,
  including the structure of DNA and inheritance patterns.
- **Understanding of Inheritance Principles:** Develop a solid understanding of the principles of
  inheritance, including Mendelian genetics, allele interactions, and the patterns of trait
  transmission across generations.
- **Application of Genetic Techniques:** Gain proficiency in the use of classical genetic
  techniques, such as Punnett squares and gene mapping, to predict and analyze inheritance
  patterns.
- **Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving:** Enhance skills in analyzing genetic data, solving
  inheritance problems, and applying genetic principles to real-world scenarios.
- **Quantitative Analysis of Genetic Data:** Gain proficiency in applying probability,
  recombination frequencies, tetrad analysis, and chi-square tests to evaluate genetic hypotheses
  and construct gene maps.
- **Genetics in Society:** Connect genetic variation and DNA technologies with human health,
  ancestry, forensic science, and the ethical, legal, and social questions raised by genetic
  information.

## Course Learning Outcomes:

Students completing this course will be able to:

- Distinguish between different types of mutations and analyze their effects on genetic disorders.
- Explain the molecular structure of DNA, including base pairing and the helical shape, to a lay
  audience.
- Identify and apply molecular genotyping techniques, such as RFLPs, VNTRs, STRs, and SNP analysis,
  in various contexts.
- Determine restriction enzyme cut locations in DNA sequences and their applications in genetic
  analysis.
- Interpret DNA fragment gel electrophoresis results for paternity testing and crime scene suspect
  identification.
- Evaluate the ethical implications of DNA profiling in forensic science and law enforcement.
- Recognize human genetic variation and assess its implications for ancestry and disease
  susceptibility.
- Identify the key stages of mitosis and meiosis, with a focus on the role of prophase I in genetic
  variation.
- Apply Mendel's four principles to solve genetic problems and predict inheritance patterns.
- Solve complex inheritance patterns using Punnett squares, including those involving multiple
  alleles and polygenic traits.
- Explain the significance of Morgan's experiments on fruit fly genetics and their contributions
  to modern genetics.
- Analyze the implications of X-linked genes for trait inheritance and their effects on offspring.
- Calculate gene distances using recombination frequencies and construct gene maps from hybrid
  cross experiments.
- Construct gene maps based on gene distances obtained from tetrad analysis.
- Assess the impact of chromosomal abnormalities, such as polyploidy and aneuploidy, on human health
  and development.
- Perform genetics &chi;2-tests to evaluate gene linkage and understand population evolution.
- Differentiate between evolutionary mechanisms, including natural selection, genetic drift, and
  gene flow, and discuss their roles in shaping genetic diversity.

## Learning Goals:

Overall, this course aims to accomplish:

- **Problem-Solving in Genetics:** Develop students' ability to approach and solve complex genetic
  problems using logical reasoning, mathematical models, and evidence-based approaches.
- **Historical Context:** Encourage an understanding of the historical development of genetic
  theories, helping students appreciate the evolution of genetic knowledge.
- **Collaborative Learning:** Foster teamwork and collaboration by encouraging group activities and
  quiz assessments, where students work together to solve complex genetic problems or analyze case
  studies.
- **Enhanced Analytical Skills:** Cultivate the ability to critically analyze genetic data and
  apply genetic principles to solve real-world problems, fostering a deep understanding of
  inheritance patterns.
- **Appreciation for Genetics:** Foster an appreciation for the complexity and elegance of genetic
  mechanisms, including how traits are passed from one generation to the next and how genetic
  variation impacts populations.
- **Lifelong Learning and Scientific Curiosity:** Encourage a lifelong interest in genetics,
  inspiring students to continue exploring the field's implications for medicine, agriculture,
  evolution, and society beyond the classroom.
- **Ethical and Social Awareness:** Promote awareness of the ethical, legal, and social implications
  of genetic research and technologies, encouraging students to consider the broader impact of
  genetics on society and human health.
