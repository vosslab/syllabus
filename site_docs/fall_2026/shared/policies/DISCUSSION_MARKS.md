# Discussion marks

## Zoom discussion marks policy

My goal is to create an engaging and interactive learning environment where everyone feels
encouraged to participate. Discussion marks are a way to recognize and reward meaningful
contributions that enhance learning.

How to earn discussion marks:

- Participate in icebreaker activities at the start of class.
- Engage in the class chat by responding to direct questions during the lecture.
- Provide thoughtful answers to open-ended questions about the material.
- Ask clarifying questions if something is unclear.
- Share personal experiences that relate to the course content.
- Post relevant news articles or resources that connect to the topics we discuss.
- Be on time for the Zoom meeting to earn discussion marks for punctuality.

What does not count:

- Simple acknowledgments like "hi," "thanks," or "yes."
- Answering polls, as it is challenging to assess this data for marks.
- Direct messages during the Zoom session; only contributions to the entire group chat count.

Earn up to 6 discussion marks per three-hour class from active chat participation. These guidelines
are designed to encourage meaningful and collaborative engagement in our virtual classroom.

## In-person discussion poker chips policy

My goal is to create an engaging and interactive classroom environment where everyone feels
encouraged to participate. Discussion marks are a way to recognize and reward meaningful
contributions that enhance our collective learning experience.

How to earn discussion marks:

- Participate in icebreaker activities at the start of class.
- Raise your hand and contribute by answering direct questions posed during the lecture.
- Provide thoughtful answers to open-ended questions about the material.
- Ask clarifying questions if something is unclear.
- Share relevant personal experiences that relate to the course content.
- Introduce interesting news articles or resources that connect to the topics we discuss.

**Tracking participation:** When you participate meaningfully, you will receive a poker chip. At
the end of the lecture, return your poker chips to me to have your discussion marks recorded.

Earn up to 6 discussion marks per 3 hour class by making valuable contributions. These guidelines
are designed to foster a meaningful and collaborative learning environment where your
contributions can help deepen everyone's understanding of the material.

## Common criticisms and my responses to discussion grades

I am fully aware of the drawbacks to this system, but I really want to encourage discussion.
Without discussion, you might as well view the lecture on YouTube. There are four main complaints
about this system, I am aware that they exist, and I actively try to reduce their impact.

1. **Students ask silly or stupid questions to get marks:** I have to encourage all questions and
   comments, because otherwise more introverted students might feel discouraged from asking a
   perceived stupid question.
2. **The instructor favors certain students:** I am aware that I tend to give marks to some
   students more than others, and I actively try to avoid this through mark shaming (discouraging
   over-eager students from getting more) and placing limits on the maximum marks, but alas, I am
   human. I appreciate feedback, so please feel free to report to me any issues of favoritism.
3. **The distribution of marks is inconsistent:** Some great questions go unrewarded while poor
   questions often get rewarded. Again, I am human; if you feel that you were overlooked and missed
   a mark (or more), please come up to me after class. I am very forgiving and will likely apologize
   and reward you for it.
4. **Creates a competitive environment for discussion that is overly stressful:** You should not
   feel that it is a competition; if you speak only once per week, you can still get a decent grade.
   Remember, using the cube root ensures that the more marks you get, the less value each additional
   mark has.

## Scoring of discussion grades

During the discussion, I will award discussion marks to students for stimulating discussion,
asking thoughtful questions, or correctly answering posed questions. **Discussion marks are not
directly equal to points in the grade book.**

At the end of the semester, I will use the fourth-highest discussion mark count as the baseline for
100%. Then, I will apply the cube root to each student's discussion marks ratio to determine their
distribution. A person with zero discussion marks will receive a minimal amount of points, and the
remaining discussion marks will be scaled up to the maximum points. Using the cube root ensures
that as you accumulate more marks, each additional mark has less impact.

There is a limit of 2 discussion marks per hour of the course, so for a 3-hour session, the limit is
6 discussion marks. This rule prevents students from hoarding discussion marks. Discussion marks
may also be rewarded for showing up to class on time or staying for the entire class, but this will
only apply if attendance becomes a problem.

## No make-up for discussion marks

I do not offer make-up opportunities for missed discussion marks. The discussion marks system
rewards meaningful participation throughout the semester. Missed opportunities to participate in
class discussions cannot be made up later. However, the grading scale accounts for different
levels of participation. Consistent engagement throughout the semester is encouraged, but active
participation in at least half of the class sessions, or partial participation in all class
sessions, still results in a high grade. By rule, more than half of the students in the class will
earn an A or A-. This system ensures fairness and highlights the importance of contributing during
live class discussions. Contributions from all students add to the collective learning experience.
