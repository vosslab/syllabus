## Learning Objectives:

Students completing this course will have achieved:

- **Statistical Foundations for Biology:** Develop a working understanding of data types,
  probability distributions, sampling variation, and the descriptive statistics used to study
  biological and health-science data.
- **Description and Visualization of Data:** Gain proficiency in using measures of center and
  variation, z-scores, percentiles, frequency distributions, box plots, and other figures to reveal
  patterns that a list of raw observations cannot show.
- **Spreadsheet-Based Quantitative Analysis:** Build transparent Google Sheets analyses in which
  the data, formulas, test statistics, p-values, and resulting decisions can be inspected and
  recalculated when observations or sample sizes change.
- **Selection and Application of Statistical Tests:** Develop the reasoning needed to match a
  biological question and study design with a Z-test, one-sample or two-sample T-test, F-test, or
  one-way ANOVA and then interpret the resulting test statistic and p-value.
- **Regression and Calibration:** Understand how a linear model, its slope and intercept, and the
  coefficient of determination can be used to evaluate a relationship or estimate an unknown from
  a biological standard curve.
- **Responsible Statistical Communication:** Recognize p-hacking, unjustified one-tailed tests,
  and unsupported causal claims, then complete an applied analysis that communicates the
  biological question, method, visual evidence, result, uncertainty, and limitations.

## Course Learning Outcomes:

Students completing this course will be able to:

- Classify variables as categorical or quantitative and distinguish discrete from continuous data
  so that the analysis matches the evidence, then calculate and interpret the mean, median, mode,
  range, variance, standard deviation, z-score, and percentile of a biological dataset.
- Describe the center, spread, shape, skew, and possible outliers of a distribution and construct
  appropriate frequency tables, histograms, box plots, or scatterplots to show those features.
- Formulate null and alternative hypotheses and justify whether a research question requires a
  one-tailed or two-tailed test before examining the result, then select a test whose assumptions
  match the sample size, population information, number of groups, and study design.
- Perform and interpret Z-tests, one-sample and two-sample T-tests, F-tests of two variances, and
  one-way ANOVA, including the appropriate test statistic, degrees of freedom, p-value, and
  reject-or-fail-to-reject decision.
- Fit a linear regression, interpret its slope, intercept, and coefficient of determination, and
  use a standard-curve equation to estimate an unknown value in a biological assay.
- Build a transparent Google Sheets analysis that recalculates when the data change, explain how
  sample size and unusual observations affect the result, identify p-hacking or overinterpretation,
  and present a defensible biological conclusion with appropriate limitations.

## Learning Goals:

Overall, this course aims to accomplish:

- **Quantitative Reasoning in Biology:** Develop the confidence to translate a biological question
  into variables, comparisons, calculations, and a conclusion supported by numerical evidence.
- **Method Selection over Recipe Following:** Emphasize why a statistical procedure fits a
  question and dataset instead of treating analysis as the memorization of formulas or software
  commands.
- **Respect for Biological Variation:** Treat variation, sampling error, uncertainty, and unusual
  observations as information to investigate rather than inconveniences to hide.
- **Transparent and Reusable Analysis:** Make each calculation and decision visible enough that
  another person can inspect the work, reproduce the result, and understand how the conclusion was
  reached.
- **Scientific Skepticism and Integrity:** Build the habit of questioning p-values, graphs,
  assumptions, causal language, and selective reporting before accepting a statistical claim.
- **Independent Communication with Data:** Prepare students to integrate prose, tables, figures,
  statistical results, and biological interpretation into a clear explanation and transfer that
  practice to new datasets in ecology, genetics, molecular biology, health science, and other
  experimental fields.
